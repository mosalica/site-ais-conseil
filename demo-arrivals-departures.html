MONDAY · 8:00 AM · Atlantic Precision Manufacturing — 240 employees, two plants

You are the IT technician.

Coffee in hand, inbox already full. A new employee starts this morning — and everything she needs to work passes through you: her account, her access, her phone, her badge, her workstation.

You will live this Monday twice: once the way it is usually done, by hand — and once with an agent preparing the work for your approval. Same day, same people. Very different outcome.

Mail — Inbox (3 unread)

Good morning,

Sophie Martin starts today as Quality Inspector on the production floor. She arrives at 8:30 AM. She will need a laptop, a phone, a door badge, and access to the quality systems.

Her HR record is attached. Thanks for getting her set up!

— Diane

One more thing: three weeks ago, an employee named Marc Desjardins left the company. Keep his name in mind. Nobody has mentioned him since.

Tip: start with the manual way. It hurts a little — that's the point.

Nothing in the second run is magic. The agent reads the same HR record you read, prepares the same tasks you did by hand, and stops at the same place a good process should stop: your judgement. What disappears is the typing, the chasing, the waiting — and the forgetting.

Arrivals and departures are where access control is won or lost. Every account created late is a morning wasted; every account closed late is a door left open. The fix is not a smarter tool. It is a prepared decision.
