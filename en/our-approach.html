Our approach

Before any project, we set the framework: what the assistant can see, what it may do on its own, and what always requires your decision. You stay in control, the assistant prepares the work — and everything it does can be checked. Here is how, in practice.

Our founding principle: the agent never replaces the human. It serves the human: it lightens their work by automating certain tasks, and always leaves the human in charge of decisions.

The foundation: five guardrails, before any feature

In short, we manage every assistant like an account with sensitive access: we know who it is, what it is allowed to do, and we keep a record of everything it does. These five guardrails are in place before the assistant touches anything at your business:

  • 1. Its own identity per assistant — each one has its own name and its own access: you always know which one did what, like an employee with their own badge.
  • 2. Minimal rights, declared in advance — each assistant can only reach the precise list of tools and documents you approved. Nothing else, never by default.
  • 3. Classified actions — what the assistant may do on its own, what requires your approval, what is forbidden. Written down before the start, not discovered afterwards.
  • 4. Human validation for the sensitive — paying, sending, changing, testing your defences: anything that matters goes through a human who decides. The assistant prepares; it does not decide.
  • 5. A complete log — everything is recorded, and you can review it: who did what, when, with which data.

The result: an assistant that refuses what falls outside its scope — including an instruction hidden in a document — rather than obeying blindly. See the refusal in action in the demo.

Your data stays with you — you keep the keys

Our agents are deployed at your place, in your systems: your data stays hosted and controlled in Canada, with no dependence on a foreign vendor's cloud. The assessment gives you the one-page sovereignty sheet — where your data lives, who you depend on, who keeps the keys. See how we protect your data.

We test your defences by playing the attacker — under a mandate, with a human deciding

Thinking like an adversary is the best way to find your weaknesses before fraudsters do — provided it is done within a written scope, with a human deciding at each step. In practice: we observe only (what is called reconnaissance), never autonomous exploitation; the test runs on a copy of your systems, never on your real tools without your written approval; then we sort alerts by severity and fix them, together.

In the lab, using fictitious examples: 62 tests passed out of 62 when playing the attacker, 18 out of 18 for alert triage. No test on your real systems without your written approval setting the scope.

Aligned with the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications (2026)

Our method is aligned with the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications (2026 edition, published by the OWASP GenAI Security Project) — the reference list of the main risks in AI applications. Our guardrails answer its top risks directly: instructions injected through documents (LLM01), sensitive information disclosure (LLM02), and excessive agency granted to the agent (LLM03) — hence out-of-scope refusal, least-privilege role access, human validation, and logging.

This is alignment, not certification: OWASP does not certify. We use this list as a checking framework, and we state honestly what is in place and what still needs to be put in place at your business.
